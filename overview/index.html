Tidy Butt is a community-focused organisation that aims to support people through connection, activity, conversation, and community engagement.

The focus is on helping people take positive steps, however small, towards improved wellbeing, confidence, and social connection.

Many people who engage with Tidy Butt may be experiencing:

  • Loneliness or isolation
  • Anxiety or low mood
  • Loss of confidence
  • Life changes or difficult circumstances
  • Mental health challenges
  • Financial or socio-economic pressures
  • Periods of crisis

Tidy Butt is not a clinical service. It is a community, a support network, and a place where people can find connection, structure, encouragement, and opportunities to move forward.

Ambassadors are a key part of making this possible.

Being a Tidy Butt volunteer or ambassador isn’t just about what you do; it’s about the impact you create.

Volunteers bring energy, passion, and a shared commitment to making mental health support more accessible.

At the heart of this are a few simple ideas:

  • Community focused – Creating safe spaces for open conversations and support.
  • Passionate about change – Helping break the stigma around mental health and supporting real change through commitment and consistency.
  • Supportive and inclusive – Whether welcoming people at events or providing a listening ear, helping people feel valued and heard.
  • Ready to grow and learn – Volunteering is also an opportunity for personal growth, learning new skills, meeting like-minded people, and making a difference.

These are not strict requirements. They are more like the mindset and values that help someone be a good ambassador.

Being an ambassador means you represent the values, behaviour, and culture of Tidy Butt when you are interacting with the public, participants, partners, and other volunteers.

At its core, being an ambassador means:

  • Being welcoming
  • Being kind
  • Being reliable
  • Being calm
  • Being respectful
  • Being inclusive
  • Being supportive without trying to “fix” people
  • Helping people feel safe and comfortable
  • Helping people access activities, support, and community

You are not there to solve everyone’s problems. You are there to help people feel that they are not facing things alone.

Some of the people you meet may be going through very difficult periods in their lives. You may hear difficult stories, see people in distress, or meet people in crisis.

It is important to understand a few key principles.

You are not a rescuer.

There is a concept sometimes called the “Rescue Triangle” or Drama Triangle, which includes the rescuer, the victim, and the persecutor.

As an ambassador, you must avoid becoming the rescuer. Trying to “save” people, take responsibility for their lives, or solve their problems can be harmful to them and to you.

Your role is to:

  • Listen
  • Support
  • Signpost
  • Encourage
  • Stay calm
  • Maintain boundaries

Not to fix, rescue, or take responsibility for someone else’s life.

You may hear about:

  • Bereavement
  • Abuse
  • Addiction
  • Suicide attempts
  • Self-harm
  • Homelessness
  • Financial crisis
  • Relationship breakdown

This can be emotionally difficult. It is important that you do not carry this alone, that you speak to the Tidy Butt team if something is worrying you, that you understand your limits, that you take breaks when needed, and that you look after your own wellbeing.

This is sometimes called vicarious trauma or secondary trauma. Hearing other people’s trauma can affect you too.

If someone is in crisis, ambassadors are not expected to manage crisis situations alone. Stay calm, stay with the person if safe to do so, and contact the appropriate support.

To be a good ambassador, you must also look after yourself.

Important boundaries include:

  • You are allowed to say no
  • You are allowed to take breaks
  • You are not available 24/7
  • You should not give out personal contact details unless agreed
  • You should not promise things you cannot deliver
  • You should not take responsibility for someone else’s safety alone
  • You should ask for help when you need it

A burnt-out ambassador cannot help anyone. A supported ambassador can help many people.

Tidy Butt is not about hierarchy and titles. It is about people working together, supporting each other, and building something that helps others.

We want ambassadors to feel:

  • Part of a team
  • Supported
  • Trusted
  • Comfortable asking questions
  • Comfortable saying when something is difficult
  • Proud of what they are part of

This is a community and a tribe effort. We support each other, learn from each other, and build something together that is bigger than any one person.

Ambassadors may be involved in different types of activities depending on their interests, confidence, and availability. These roles may overlap, and people may do more than one.

Not everyone has to do everything. People can contribute in ways that suit their skills, confidence, and personal situation.
